# Chapter 1 Complete

*Chapter 1 · Wrap-Up.*

That was the grand tour. You can now:

- **Say what an OS is**, from the user's point of view and the system's: a resource allocator and a control program, built around a kernel that's always running.
- **Follow the hardware**: the bootstrap program, device controllers on a shared bus, interrupts and the interrupt vector, synchronous and asynchronous I/O, and DMA.
- **Climb the storage hierarchy** from tape to registers, and explain what caching does and why copies must stay coherent.
- **Tell architectures apart**: single-processor, symmetric and asymmetric multiprocessing, multicore, and clusters.
- **Explain how the OS stays in charge**: multiprogramming and timesharing, dual-mode operation, the mode bit, system calls, and the timer. You built a machine that enforces them.
- **Name the OS's management jobs**: processes, memory, files, mass storage, and I/O, plus protection and security.
- **Pick the right kernel data structure**, and you built a bitmap and a binary search tree to prove why balance matters.
- **Place an OS in its environment**: mobile, client-server, peer-to-peer, virtualized, cloud, real-time embedded, and open source.

Every topic here was a preview. Processes, scheduling, synchronization, memory, storage, I/O, and protection each get a full chapter as the course grows.
