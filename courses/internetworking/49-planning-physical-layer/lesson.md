# Planning the Physical Layer: Site and Space

*Chapter 2 · Topologies and the Physical Layer.*

Before a single cable is bought, someone has to decide where the network lives, what it's made of, and who can reach it. Mistakes here are the most expensive to fix later: you can reconfigure a router in minutes, but moving a data center takes months.

The planning questions fall into four groups. This lesson covers the first two: the site and the space. The next covers devices, access, and keeping things running.

## 1. The site

- **Real estate location**: Is the site exposed to **natural disasters** (floods, earthquakes, hurricanes)? To **other disasters** (a chemical plant next door, a flight path, unreliable power)?
- **How much space?** Enough for today, plus room to grow.
- **On-site vs remote**: keep the servers in your own building, or rent space in someone else's data center or the cloud? On-site means more control and more responsibility. Remote means less to maintain but depends on the network link to reach it.
- **Public or private requirements**: Will customers or the public visit the site? Are there laws or contracts about where the data has to live?

## 2. The space itself

- **HVAC** (heating, ventilation, air conditioning): servers turn electricity into heat, and they need cooling all day, every day.
- **Racks**: the standard metal frames that servers, switches, and patch panels bolt into.
- **Wiring closet**: the small room on each floor where that floor's cables meet a switch. Remember the 100 m segment limit: every desk on the floor has to be within reach of it.
- **Wired, wireless, or hybrid**: Wired is faster and more secure. Wireless is cheaper to deploy and lets people move. Most sites are a hybrid: wired for desks and servers, wireless for laptops and phones.
