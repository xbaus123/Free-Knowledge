# Course Complete

*Wrap-Up · The Whole Course.*

You made it. That was the last quiz.

When you started, the Internet was something that just worked, or didn't. Now you can open it up. You can:

- **Follow a click** from the radio in your laptop to a server across the world, and name every protocol on the way.
- **Subnet** a network in your head, and spot an address that's on the wrong side of a mask.
- **Read a routing table**, and a `traceroute`, and explain why the packet went where it did.
- **Debug DNS** with `dig`, and read the Received: headers of a suspicious email.
- **Design a network** from the cables in the walls to the sensors on the ceiling, and plan for the day part of it breaks.

## Your project

If you've followed the class project, you've designed a complete network: rooms and cables, VLANs, subnets, routing, DNS, mail, a website, a resilience plan, and wireless. That's a real piece of work. Keep it. It's the kind of thing worth showing someone.

## Where to go next

- **Get your hands dirty.** Capture your own traffic with Wireshark and find the DNS lookup, the TCP handshake, and the TLS hello. Build your design in Cisco Packet Tracer. Turn an old router or a Raspberry Pi into a home lab and run your own DNS server.
- **Read the source.** Chapter 1 met the RFCs. Now you can read them: RFC 791 (IPv4), RFC 9293 (TCP), RFCs 1034 and 1035 (DNS), RFC 5321 (SMTP), RFC 9110 (HTTP). They're shorter and clearer than their reputation.
- **Certify it**, if that helps your career. CompTIA Network+ and Cisco's CCNA cover most of this course, and Security+ is a natural next step after chapters 10 and 11.
- **Keep learning here.** The **System Design** course builds on everything you know now: load balancers, caches, and the trade-offs of running services at scale.

## One last thing

Chapter 1 described the Internet as a network of networks with **no central hub**. Nobody owns it. It works because thousands of independent people agreed on open standards, and published them for anyone to read.

That includes you now. The Internet isn't a black box anymore. It's layers, and you know every one of them.

Thanks for learning with us. **Course complete.**
